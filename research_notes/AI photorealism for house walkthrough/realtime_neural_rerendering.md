# Real-time / near-real-time neural re-rendering of 3D-engine frames into photoreal images (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: written for "Ridgeline Residence" (three.js r128, single static HTML page on Vercel, no server, desktop + phones, drag-and-drop furniture, engine can emit depth / normals / object-ID masks / line art).

Method caveat for the report writer: during this research the fetch tool could reach github.com but was blocked from arxiv.org, huggingface.co, nvidia.com, decart.ai and mintlify.com. Numbers attributed to those domains came from search-engine extracts of those pages, not from a full read. Where a figure appears only on aggregator or review sites, it is flagged as such.

## 1. Fast diffusion/flow approaches for render-to-photo: what exists, latency, hardware, resolution

### Takeaway
Single-frame img2img/ControlNet pipelines built on 1–4-step distilled models (SD-Turbo, LCM-LoRA, SDXL-Turbo/Lightning, FLUX.2 [klein]) run from tens of fps at 512 px on an RTX 4090 down to about 0.2–2 s per 1 MP image. Streaming video-diffusion systems (StreamDiffusionV2, Krea Realtime 14B) reach 11–64 fps at about 480p, but only on datacenter GPUs (B200, or 4×H100). Quality rises and speed falls in this order: SD-Turbo/SD1.5-class, then SDXL-class, then FLUX-class.

### Cited Findings
**Image-model pipelines (frame-by-frame img2img)**
- StreamDiffusion v1 (Dec 2023, older but still the baseline) benchmark on RTX 4090 + i9-13900K: SD-Turbo with 1 step reaches 106.16 fps txt2img and 93.897 fps img2img. LCM-LoRA + KohakuV2 with 4 steps reaches 38.023 fps txt2img and 37.133 fps img2img. The benchmark resolution is not stated; the img2img example resizes to 512×512. — [StreamDiffusion GitHub](https://github.com/cumulo-autumn/StreamDiffusion)
- StreamDiffusion accelerates with TensorRT plus the tiny TAESD VAE, and averages inference time over 100 images. — [StreamDiffusion paper, arXiv 2312.12491](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.12491)
- StreamDiffusion's Residual CFG cuts guidance cost from about 2N (regular CFG) to about N (Self-Negative) or N+1 (Onetime-Negative). The Stochastic Similarity Filter skips processing when consecutive frames are similar, up to a maximum skip interval. — [StreamDiffusion GitHub](https://github.com/cumulo-autumn/StreamDiffusion)
- Community wrapper TouchDiffusion: 55–60 fps on a 4090 with sd-turbo at 512×512, batch 1. It does not say whether ControlNet was on. — [TouchDiffusion GitHub](https://github.com/olegchomp/TouchDiffusion)
- The Livepeer/Daydream fork of StreamDiffusion (Apache-2.0) adds SDXL support (with `taesdxl` and TensorRT), multiple TensorRT-accelerated ControlNets, IP-Adapters (including FaceID), TemporalNet v2 (optical-flow ControlNet), StreamV2V cached attention, a `latent_feedback` processor, and runtime prompt/seed blending. The repo publishes no ControlNet or SDXL fps figures. Development moved from livepeer/StreamDiffusion (archived) to daydreamlive/StreamDiffusion. — [livepeer/StreamDiffusion](https://github.com/livepeer/StreamDiffusion); [daydreamlive/StreamDiffusion](https://github.com/daydreamlive/StreamDiffusion)
- Daydream (Nov 2025) announced StreamDiffusion SDXL in its hosted API and Playground. It has accelerated HED, Depth, Pose, Tile and Canny ControlNets with multi-ControlNet support, and claims 15–25 FPS "even with complex model configurations" via TensorRT. This is a vendor claim with no independent benchmark found. — [BusinessWire press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251106860538/en/Daydream-Launches-Scope-and-Expands-StreamDiffusion-with-SDXL-Support-Advancing-the-Open-Source-Real-Time-AI-Video-Ecosystem); [Livepeer docs: Daydream gateway](https://docs.livepeer.org/v2/gateways/using-gateways/gateway-providers/daydream-gateway)
- A May 2024 changelog for the StreamDiffusionTD TouchDesigner operator said ControlNet then worked only with SD1.5 and without TensorRT. This is older and likely superseded by the Daydream fork. — [dotsimulate Patreon changelog](https://www.patreon.com/dotsimulate/posts/streamdiffusiont-104339324)
- SDXL-Turbo (Nov 2023, older): 207 ms on an A100 at 512×512 for prompt encoding + 1 denoising step + decoding in fp16. That is a text-to-image figure, not img2img. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/stable-diffusion-xl-turbo-generates-ai-images-in-real-time/)
- SDXL-Turbo img2img runs `int(num_inference_steps × strength)` steps, and the product must be ≥ 1. A single-step img2img therefore needs strength 1.0, which effectively regenerates the image. — [HF Diffusers SDXL-Turbo docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/using-diffusers/sdxl_turbo.md); [sdxl-turbo README](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/sdxl-turbo/blob/2c4b5b9e8c65b03b4cd101620ca468508e51f677/README.md)
- SDXL-Lightning: under 1 s at 4 steps on an RTX 4090 with "90–95% of base SDXL quality" (secondary guide, methodology not stated). OctoAI docs say under 1 s for 4–8 steps (GPU unnamed). Runflow's hosted API returns in about 1–2 s including API overhead. — [ucstrategies guide](https://ucstrategies.com/news/sdxl-lightning-speed-benchmarks-4-step-setup-lora-guide-2026/); [OctoAI docs](https://octo.ai/docs/media-gen-solution/optimizations/sdxl-lightning); [Runflow](https://www.runflow.io/models/bytedance/sd-xl-lightning)
- FLUX.2 [klein] (released Jan 15, 2026): Black Forest Labs says it "generate[s] or edit[s] images in under 0.5s on modern hardware". Variants are 4B (Apache-2.0) and 9B (non-commercial), each in distilled and base versions. Reported 4B VRAM conflicts: about 8 GB (GitHub README / HackerNoon) versus about 13 GB (BFL blog). An aggregator claims about 2 s on an RTX 5090 and sub-second on a GB200 (unverified). — [BFL blog](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux2-klein-towards-interactive-visual-intelligence); [HackerNoon](https://sia.hackernoon.com/the-8gb-vram-image-model-that-feels-instant-meet-flux2-klein-4b); [GetApp listing](https://www.getapp.com.au/software/2250756/flux-2-klein)
- FLUX.1-dev on an H100 at 1024×1024: about 6.75 s with the stock diffusers bf16 pipeline. Modal's optimized pipeline targets under 3 s. — [Modal blog](https://frontend.modal.com/blog/flux-3x-faster); [Modal docs](https://modal.com/docs/examples/flux.md)
- Nunchaku/SVDQuant 4-bit FLUX.1 (12B): about 3.0× faster than the NF4 W4A16 baseline on a desktop RTX 4090 and about 3.5× less memory than 16-bit. On a 16 GB laptop 4090, removing CPU offload gives 8.7× over 16-bit (10.1× total). No absolute seconds were found for schnell. — [Nunchaku GitHub](https://github.com/nunchaku-ai/nunchaku); [MIT Han Lab blog](https://hanlab.mit.edu/blog/svdquant-nvfp4)
- FLUX.1 Kontext [dev] (reference-image editing): NVIDIA and BFL optimized it with TensorRT plus low-precision quantization for "near real-time editing" on RTX. No per-image seconds were found. — [NVIDIA dev blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/optimizing-flux-1-kontext-for-image-editing-with-low-precision-quantization)
- fal.ai prices FLUX.1 [schnell] at about $0.003 per megapixel (aggregator-reported; rounding rules unclear). fal offers queue REST plus realtime WebSocket/SSE surfaces. No measured WebSocket ms latency was found. — [gurubase fal FAQ](https://gurubase.io/g/fal-ai/price-of-flux1-on-fal-ai); [API Evangelist fal profile](https://providers.apievangelist.com/providers/fal-ai/)

**Streaming video-diffusion pipelines (video-to-video)**
- StreamDiffusionV2 (UC Berkeley/MIT/Stanford et al.; arXiv 2511.07399, MLSys 2026, Best Research Paper there): on 4× H100 with no TensorRT or quantization, the 14B model reaches about 58 FPS and the 1.3B model about 64.5 FPS, with first frame in about 0.5 s. With more denoising steps it sustains 31.62 FPS (14B) and 61.57 FPS (1.3B). The design is training-free and uses short chunks plus pipeline parallelism across denoising steps and layers. — [arXiv 2511.07399](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07399v2); [MLSys 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2026/file/698cfaf72a208aef2e78bcac55b74328-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- A third-party report on StreamDiffusionV2 (unverified against the paper): 480p, Wan2.1-1.3B at 42.26 FPS with 0.37 s time-to-first-frame at 1 step, and about 16 FPS on 4× RTX 4090 over PCIe. — [vllm-omni GitHub issue](https://goodfirstissue.org/vllm-project/vllm-omni/issues/2280)
- The StreamDiffusionV2 repo is video-to-video (input video + text prompt). Its examples run at 480×832 with 2 steps on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B; a 14B checkpoint is used for offline demos. A "fast" config enables the TAEHV decoder and TensorRT. Timeline: public release 2025-10-06, ring-buffer KV cache 2026-03-06, PyPI 2026-03-27, initial Blackwell support 2026-05-17. — [StreamDiffusionV2 GitHub](https://github.com/chenfengxu714/StreamDiffusionV2)
- Krea Realtime 14B (Oct 2025): distilled from Wan 2.1 14B with Self-Forcing. It reaches 11 fps text-to-video at 4 steps on one B200. The repo recommends a 40 GB+ GPU (KV cache up to 25 GB), offers a `v2v.py` video-to-video script, and examples run at 832×480. License is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, which is non-commercial. — [krea-ai/realtime-video GitHub](https://github.com/krea-ai/realtime-video); [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/krea/krea-realtime-video)
- StreamV2V (ICLR 2025) runs at about 20 FPS on one A100 and is 15×, 46×, 108× and 158× faster than FlowVid, CoDeF, Rerender-A-Video and TokenFlow respectively. In other words, Rerender and TokenFlow are offline methods. — [arXiv 2405.15757](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15757); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/7280f65ed571b7b28321f2c7cf4c60c8-Abstract-Conference.html)

**G-buffer-conditioned neural renderers (research)**
- NVIDIA DiffusionRenderer (CVPR 2025): an inverse video-diffusion model estimates G-buffers, and a forward model generates photoreal images from G-buffers "without explicit light transport simulation". Demonstrated uses are relighting, material editing, and object insertion done in G-buffer space with realistic shadows and reflections. Repo inference scripts run 24 frames at 512×512 with 20 steps, i.e. offline. — [NVIDIA project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/publication/2025_cvpr_diffusionrenderer); [arXiv 2501.18590](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18590); [GitHub nv-tlabs/diffusion-renderer](https://github.com/nv-tlabs/diffusion-renderer)

### Inferences
- For a 3D app that already has exact depth, normals and segmentation, the most direct fit is "SD-Turbo/SDXL-Turbo/Lightning + ControlNet (depth + canny/lineart, optionally seg) at 1–4 steps." On a single RTX 4090-class GPU this should land in the 10–60 fps range at 512 px, or roughly 0.2–1 s per 1 MP still. The ControlNet branch adds cost; no published depth-ControlNet fps number was found.
- FLUX-class models (FLUX.2 klein, Kontext) give better photorealism but are about 0.5–3 s per image even on top GPUs. They are good candidates for the "photo after the camera stops" still, not for per-frame streaming.
- StreamDiffusionV2 and Krea Realtime prove 480p streaming video diffusion is possible. They need 40 GB+ or multi-H100 hardware, and Krea's license blocks commercial use, so they are research/self-host options only for a funded server tier.
- DiffusionRenderer is the conceptually ideal "G-buffer → photo" model, but it is offline and research-grade.

### Gaps
- No published fps/latency for SD-Turbo or SDXL-Turbo with a depth ControlNet on an RTX 4090, L40S or H100 was found. It needs a local benchmark (StreamDiffusion's `examples/benchmark/single.py` plus a ControlNet).
- No absolute latency for FLUX.1 schnell under Nunchaku, or for FLUX.1 Kontext [dev], was found.
- L40S numbers were not found for any of these pipelines.
- Measured fal.ai WebSocket latency was not found.

## 2. Real-time video-to-video / world-model products: can they take a three.js canvas stream? APIs, pricing, latency, availability

### Takeaway
Decart's realtime API (MirageLSD, then the Lucy Restyle/Lucy 2.x line) is the only production, pay-as-you-go service found that restyles an arbitrary live video stream over WebRTC from the browser. It claims about 20–30 fps, about 40–100 ms latency, and $0.01–0.02 per second at 720p. Daydream (hosted StreamDiffusion with ControlNets) and Krea's live-viewport feature are the other usable options. NVIDIA DLSS 5 is the closest technical analog (engine frame + buffers to photoreal, one step, about 8 ms at 4K) but runs only in native RTX-50 games. Genie 3, Runway GWM-1 and Odyssey-2 generate worlds rather than re-render your geometry.

### Inferences
- A three.js canvas can be turned into a WebRTC MediaStream with the standard `canvas.captureStream(fps)` and handed to Decart's realtime JS client the same way a webcam stream is. Nothing in Decart's docs (as found) restricts input to cameras, and MirageLSD explicitly targets "a computer screen or game". This should be validated with a test, because the SDK may expect specific resolutions or fps.
- Cost: Lucy Restyle 2 at $0.01/s is $0.60 per minute, or $36 per hour per concurrent viewer. Lucy 2.1 at $0.02/s doubles that. Continuous streaming for public visitors is expensive. Metering only while a visitor holds an "AI view" toggle, or only for stills, keeps cost bounded.
- Because the site is a static Vercel page, using Decart (or fal, or Daydream) needs at least one serverless function to mint ephemeral keys or proxy requests. Vercel Functions would suffice.
- These video restylers condition on RGB frames and text prompts. They do not take your exact depth or segmentation buffers, so geometric fidelity depends on the model's own structure-preservation (see Q6). Daydream/StreamDiffusion with depth ControlNet can take engine depth directly.
- DLSS 5 shows the "right" architecture (engine frame + motion vectors, then a one-step diffusion with masking and intensity controls) but is unusable from WebGL/WebGPU in a browser. It is a native RTX-50 game feature.
- Genie 3, GWM-1 and Odyssey generate new content from prompts and actions. None of the sources describe feeding them an external engine render to re-skin, so they are not suitable for preserving an exact floor plan.

- Exact resolution, fps and end-to-end latency for Lucy Restyle 2 and mirage_v2, and whether Mirage is still sold (absent from the pricing table), could not be verified from Decart's primary docs (fetch blocked).
- No Daydream API pricing was found.
- Krea's API availability and pricing for third-party apps (as opposed to the Krea web app) was not found.
- No source describes Runway GWM-1 or Odyssey-2 accepting an external video feed for restyling.

## 3. Temporal consistency / flicker during camera motion

### Takeaway
Per-frame image diffusion flickers. Production systems fight it with three tools: causal or autoregressive video models with KV caches or feature banks (StreamV2V, StreamDiffusionV2, Krea Self-Forcing, Decart's diffusion forcing + history augmentation); engine motion vectors and optical flow (DLSS 5, TemporalNet, latent and noise warping); and skipping work when the input hasn't changed. Offline methods (Rerender-A-Video, TokenFlow) are 100×+ too slow for interactive use.

### Cited Findings
- StreamV2V keeps a feature bank of past frames' keys and values, continually merged to stay compact. It runs at about 20 FPS on an A100, and metrics plus user studies confirm temporal consistency. It is 108× faster than Rerender and 158× faster than TokenFlow. — [arXiv 2405.15757](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15757)
- The Daydream StreamDiffusion fork integrates StreamV2V ("caches attention from previous frames to reduce flickering, without batching or fine-tuning"), TemporalNet v2 (optical-flow ControlNet) and a `latent_feedback` processor. — [livepeer/StreamDiffusion](https://github.com/livepeer/StreamDiffusion); [daydreamlive/StreamDiffusion](https://github.com/daydreamlive/StreamDiffusion)
- StreamDiffusionV2 uses a causal DiT with a ring-buffer KV cache for sliding-window attention (added Mar 2026). — [StreamDiffusionV2 GitHub](https://github.com/chenfengxu714/StreamDiffusionV2)
- Krea Realtime uses Self-Forcing to make Wan 2.1 autoregressive, with optimized KV-cache management. — [krea-ai/realtime-video](https://github.com/krea-ai/realtime-video)
- MirageLSD: errors in autoregressive video compound, and one account says that after about 30 s accumulated errors "seriously degrade" quality. Decart uses diffusion forcing (denoise without relying on earlier frames) and history augmentation (training on corrupted past frames so the model learns to correct them). — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/decart-launches-miragelsd-an-ai-model-that-transforms-live-video-feeds-in-real-time)
- DLSS 5 keeps results stable as the camera moves by running strictly one frame in, one frame out using game-engine motion vectors. — [NVIDIA dev blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/whats-new-for-game-developers-dlss-5-with-3d-guided-neural-rendering-nvidia-ace-updates-and-new-rtx-kit-capabilities/); [NVIDIA ADLR](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/adlr/DLSS5/)
- Noise warping: "How I Warped Your Noise" (integral noise, flow-based warping) reduces high-frequency flicker and texture sticking. Infinite-Resolution Integral Noise Warping (2025) is training-free. Go-with-the-Flow (2025) does real-time noise warping, plus fine-tuning of video diffusion on warped noise. — [Survey of Video Diffusion Models, arXiv 2504.16081](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16081v3)
- Latent warping: DiffIR2VR-Zero warps keyframe latents with GMFlow optical flow plus occlusion masks, then propagates them within batches. MoVideo combines flow with depth to handle occlusions. Text2Video-Zero and Rerender-A-Video warp latents across frames. — [DiffIR2VR-Zero, arXiv 2407.01519](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01519); [MoVideo ECCV 2024](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/06030.pdf); [Survey arXiv 2504.16081](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16081v3)
- StreamDiffusion's Stochastic Similarity Filter skips diffusion when consecutive input frames are similar. This saves GPU time and also avoids re-sampling (and re-flickering) a static view. — [StreamDiffusion GitHub](https://github.com/cumulo-autumn/StreamDiffusion)
- Genie 3 maintains about 1 minute of visual memory, so an object looked away from and back to keeps its state. Genie 2 managed only about 10–20 s of coherence. — [etcentric](https://www.etcentric.org/?p=195104); [opencv.org](https://opencv.org/genie-3/)

### Inferences
- A three.js engine can supply exact per-pixel motion vectors and depth, which beats estimated optical flow. That makes flow-based latent and noise warping, or DLSS-5-style motion-vector reprojection, more reliable here than in the video-restyling literature, which has to estimate flow from RGB.
- A fixed seed plus a fixed prompt plus strong structural ControlNets reduces, but does not remove, frame-to-frame texture "boiling" in single-image pipelines. This is commonly reported by practitioners, but no quantitative source was found (see Gaps).
- Keyframe + warp is the cheapest pattern for walk mode: generate an AI still at a keyframe, reproject it to nearby camera poses using engine depth, and regenerate only disoccluded regions or when the pose drift exceeds a threshold. No product doing exactly this for browser 3D was found.

### Gaps
- No published quantitative flicker metric (e.g. warp error) comparing fixed-seed SD-Turbo+ControlNet against StreamV2V/TemporalNet on synthetic 3D walkthroughs was found.
- No published demo of DLSS-5-style motion-vector-guided diffusion inside WebGL/WebGPU was found.

## 4. Running in the browser on-device (WebGPU / ONNX Runtime Web / WebNN / transformers.js)

### Takeaway
WebGPU now ships on all major desktop browsers, Safari on iOS/iPadOS 26, and Chrome on Android, so the runtime exists. But browser diffusion is still roughly 1 s per 512 px SD-Turbo image on an RTX 4090 and several to tens of seconds on laptops and integrated GPUs. Real-time in-browser re-rendering on phones is not feasible in 2026. An occasional 512 px "AI photo" on a strong desktop GPU is borderline feasible.

### Cited Findings
- ONNX Runtime Web 1.17 (Feb 2024, older) launched WebGPU and demoed SD-Turbo in the browser. Microsoft reported output within about 1 s on an RTX 4090. — [Microsoft Open Source blog](https://opensource.microsoft.com/blog/2024/02/29/onnx-runtime-web-unleashes-generative-ai-in-the-browser-using-webgpu/); [@IT (Japanese) summary](https://atmarkit.itmedia.co.jp/ait/articles/2403/06/news067.html)
- A Hugging Face Spaces demo (webai-community/demos) hosts an SD-Turbo WebGPU page; no timing data. — [HF Spaces commit](https://huggingface.co/spaces/webai-community/demos/commit/78e34bca42e242f408e93d99e1585f9f278cf42e)
- A self-reported browser SD 1.5 model card (512×512, 20 steps) gives about 3–8 s on an RTX 4090/4080, 10–20 s on an M2 Max/M1 Ultra, and 25–50 s on Intel Iris Xe. — [Zhare-AI/sd-1-5-webgpu](https://huggingface.co/Zhare-AI/sd-1-5-webgpu)
- An Oct 8, 2026 GitHub spike proposes SDXS-512 and SD-Turbo on WebGPU via a stable-diffusion.cpp core. It was tested only on Chromium 153 on an Apple M4 Max, with component timings (e.g. GROUP_NORM 0.43 s → 0.34 s) but no full-pipeline time. It was not tested on Safari, Firefox, Windows or Android. — [llamadart issue #969](https://github.com/leehack/llamadart/issues/969)
- A 2026 WebNN + ONNX Runtime write-up claims turbo models (SD-Turbo, LCM) run in about 100–300 ms with GPU acceleration, with no hardware or method stated. This is unverified and inconsistent with the other data points. — [scribbler.live](https://scribbler.live/2026/04/02/Stable-Diffusion-in-the-Browser-with-WebNN-ONNX.html)
- A Z-Image-Turbo ONNX build targets the browser via WebNN and WebGPU, with a live WebNN demo. No timings were found. — [lwanming/Z-Image-Turbo-opt](https://huggingface.co/lwanming/Z-Image-Turbo-opt)
- WebGPU is on by default in Safari on iOS 26, iPadOS 26, macOS Tahoe 26 and visionOS 26. Chrome on Android supports it. Firefox Linux/Android/Intel-Mac support is "in progress", with Android targeted for late 2026. — [web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers); [App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/webgpu-in-ios-26/)
- Native (not browser) on-phone generation: SnapGen (379M params, CVPR 2025) takes 1.2–2.3 s for 1024×1024, about 1.4 s on an iPhone 16 Pro Max. Earlier, Qualcomm's SD on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 took under 15 s. The 2026 Galaxy S26 Creative Studio uses cloud servers for image generation. — [Snap newsroom](https://newsroom.snap.com/ai-text-to-image-model-for-mobile-devices); [CVPR 2025 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Chen_SnapGen_Taming_High-Resolution_Text-to-Image_Models_for_Mobile_Devices_with_Efficient_CVPR_2025_paper.html); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Qualcomm-brings-art-generating-AI-to-smartphones-with-on-device-Smart-Diffusion-demo.697017.0.html); [PromptQuorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/local-llms/galaxy-s26-local-ai-on-device-2026)
- FLUX.2 [klein] 4B needs about 8–13 GB VRAM natively (conflicting sources). — [BFL blog](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux2-klein-towards-interactive-visual-intelligence)

### Inferences
- Model download size is a practical blocker for a static Vercel page. SD-Turbo-class ONNX weights are around 1–2 GB (my estimate from typical SD1.x/2.x fp16 checkpoint sizes; not sourced in this research), FLUX-class far more. First-visit download and WebGPU shader compilation dominate.
- Running diffusion alongside a three.js r128 WebGL renderer on the same phone GPU would contend for GPU time and memory. Phones (and integrated-GPU laptops) should use a cloud endpoint. On-device should be at most an opt-in desktop "experimental" path.
- three.js r128 is WebGL-only; that is fine, since a WebGPU inference runtime can coexist and read pixels or depth via canvas readback.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2026 measurement of SD-Turbo, SDXS or SDXL-Turbo end-to-end in a browser on a laptop iGPU, M-series Mac or 2026 flagship phone was found.
- No browser ControlNet (depth) timing was found.
- Transformers.js-specific diffusion benchmarks were not found.

## 5. Hybrid UX patterns: live raster while moving, AI "photo" after the camera stops

### Takeaway
Shipping architecture-visualization products already separate the real-time viewport from AI image generation. Chaos Veras (now inside Enscape, V-Ray and Corona) generates AI images from the live model's geometry and camera, and Krea Realtime mirrors a live 3D viewport. Systems like StreamDiffusion skip work when the input frame is static. No product was found that auto-fades an AI still in 1–3 s after the camera stops in a web 3D viewer, though this mirrors the project's existing progressive-accumulation "Photo-real" mode.

### Cited Findings
- Chaos (May 27, 2026) integrated Veras into every version of Enscape, V-Ray and Corona. Veras "uses the actual 3D geometry and camera views from Enscape, so AI renders are grounded in the real model". Veras 4 (Feb 2026) moved to an AI rendering engine powered by Google's Nano Banana Pro. Usage is metered via "Chaos Credits". It is positioned for conceptual and design stages, not final production. — [Chaos press](https://www.chaos.com/press/veras-ai-architecture-enscape-vray-corona); [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/05/29/chaos-puts-veras-everywhere/); [NTI](https://www.nti-group.com/uk/products/other-products/chaos/veras/); [Architosh](https://architosh.com/2026/05/enscape-v-ray-and-corona-get-veras-ai-integration/)
- Veras generates images from a viewport or model snapshot, so it sits alongside the real-time viewport rather than replacing it. — [cadscene](https://cadscene.com/enscape-ai-rendering)
- Krea Realtime mirrors a live 3D-tool viewport into AI images "without exporting a new still". A third-party desktop client captures a screen region (e.g. the Blender viewport) as live input. — [Krea for Architecture](https://www.krea.ai/solutions/architecture); [Krea Realtime Edit blog](https://www.krea.ai/blog/realtime-edit); [krea-ai-realtime-edit GitHub (unofficial)](https://github.com/KREA-AI-realtime/krea-ai-realtime-edit)
- StreamDiffusion's Stochastic Similarity Filter pauses processing when frames are similar. — [StreamDiffusion GitHub](https://github.com/cumulo-autumn/StreamDiffusion)
- DLSS 5 exposes an intensity control and masking. This is a precedent for a blend slider between raster and AI output and for excluding regions from the effect. — [NVIDIA dev blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/whats-new-for-game-developers-dlss-5-with-3d-guided-neural-rendering-nvidia-ace-updates-and-new-rtx-kit-capabilities/)

### Inferences
- Recommended pattern for Ridgeline:
  1. While orbiting, walking or dragging furniture, show the existing rasterized and progressive "Photo-real" view.
  2. On a camera/scene idle of about 300–500 ms, capture color + depth + normals + ID mask + line art and send them to a hosted 1–4-step SDXL/FLUX-klein ControlNet endpoint.
  3. Cross-fade the returned image (about 0.5–3 s) over the canvas.
  4. Cancel or ignore stale requests whenever the camera or scene changes.
  5. Optionally reproject the last AI frame with engine depth for small moves.

  This reuses the same "settle then refine" mental model as the existing progressive accumulation.
- Cache AI stills keyed by (camera pose, scene-state hash), so revisiting a viewpoint is free. Pre-generate hero viewpoints offline at high quality.
- For paid or demo sessions, a "Live AI" toggle can stream through Decart or Daydream with explicit cost metering.

### Gaps
- No public evidence of a web 3D configurator (three.js/Babylon) shipping an auto-fade-in AI still on camera stop was found.
- Krea's and Veras's measured seconds per render are not published (only "in seconds" marketing).
- Vizcom, SketchUp Diffusion, D5 AI and Lumion AI were not covered by the results.

## 6. Geometric fidelity: do these methods move or hallucinate objects? Conditioning-strength guidance

### Takeaway
Fidelity is best when the model is conditioned on structure maps (depth + edges/line art, optionally segmentation) rather than plain img2img or text-plus-RGB restyling. Even then, depth-only ControlNet can drop or alter furniture, and no rigorous hallucination benchmark exists. Practitioner guidance is a depth ControlNet scale of about 0.65–0.75, with collapse risk below 0.5. Combining depth with canny or lineart, and verifying against the engine's ID mask, is the practical safeguard. DLSS 5-style "start from the rendered frame" models are designed to avoid reinterpretation.

### Cited Findings
- LooseControl (SIGGRAPH 2024) reports that plain depth ControlNet "often yields 'empty' rooms" across conditioning scales, with weak prompt adherence. Box-like approximate depth yields only boxy furniture. — [LooseControl, ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3641519.3657525)
- Interior-design practitioner guide: `controlnet_conditioning_scale` = 0.7 is a reasonable default. Values below 0.5 "sometimes cause spatial collapse in interior layouts". Test 0.65–0.75 and always validate output geometry against the reference. These are heuristics, not measurements. — [theneuralbase ControlNet interior guide](https://theneuralbase.com/controlnet/learn/intermediate/interior-design-visualization/); [agentbus guide](https://agentbus.sh/posts/how-to-build-ai-interior-design-rendering-with-controlnet/)
- An architecture course page frames img2img denoise strength as a tolerance: tighter geometry requirements mean lower strength. Depth captures surface distance, and canny captures hard edges of walls, doorways and furniture outlines, so combining them stabilizes outlines. — [studiomatrx: img2img & ControlNet, keep your geometry](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/ai-ml-for-architects/img2img-controlnet-architecture)
- Estimated depth maps fail on mirrors and glass, and muddy depth (walls blending into floors) propagates into the render. — [agentbus guide](https://agentbus.sh/posts/how-to-build-ai-interior-design-rendering-with-controlnet/)
- A style-transfer study found depth among the least restrictive conditioning types (best scale 0.25 for ArtFID). That optimizes style, not fidelity. — [Scheduled Style Injection, arXiv 2605.26538](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26538)
- Single-step SDXL-Turbo img2img needs strength 1.0 (steps × strength ≥ 1), i.e. full regeneration. Structure must therefore come from ControlNet, not from low img2img strength, when using 1-step models. — [HF Diffusers SDXL-Turbo docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/main/en/using-diffusers/sdxl_turbo.md)
- DLSS 5 starts from the engine's rendered frame and is "not supposed to generate an entirely new interpretation of a scene", with masking and intensity controls. Critics still raise artistic-fidelity concerns. — [NVIDIA dev blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/whats-new-for-game-developers-dlss-5-with-3d-guided-neural-rendering-nvidia-ace-updates-and-new-rtx-kit-capabilities/); [eesel review](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/dlss-5-review)
- DiffusionRenderer inserts objects (a bathtub, a table) in G-buffer space, and the forward renderer produces matching shadows and reflections. This shows G-buffer conditioning preserves inserted-object placement. — [NVIDIA DiffusionRenderer](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/toronto-ai/publication/2025_cvpr_diffusionrenderer)
- Veras is marketed as "grounded in the real model" via actual geometry and camera. — [NTI](https://www.nti-group.com/uk/products/other-products/chaos/veras/)
- Lucy Restyle is marketed as "identity-safe" video-to-video restyling. — [RunComfy](https://www.runcomfy.com/models/decart/lucy-edit/restyle)

### Inferences
- Ridgeline has a structural advantage: engine-rendered depth and normals are exact, with no estimator errors on glass or mirrors, and the ID mask gives per-object ground truth. Recommended conditioning stack:
  - depth (about 0.7–0.9) + lineart/canny from engine edges (about 0.5–0.8) + optional segmentation or ID colors;
  - the raster color frame as the img2img init where step count allows (e.g. 4-step Lightning/LCM at strength about 0.5–0.7);
  - prompt text auto-built from the object list (e.g. "walnut dining table, boucle sofa, wool rug").
  These are starting points to tune, not published optima.
- Add a post-check. Run a cheap segmentation or edge comparison between the AI output and the engine ID mask or line art, and fall back to the raster frame (or blend per object using the ID mask) where IoU drops. Per-object blending also lets small art and lamp details come from the raster.
- RGB-only realtime restylers (Decart, Krea viewport) give no hard geometry guarantee. Expect occasional object changes or texture drift, especially for small items (lamps, art), and over long sessions (Decart reports error buildup after about 30 s without mitigation).

### Gaps
- No quantitative study was found measuring object displacement or hallucination rates versus ControlNet scale for interior renders.
- No published recommended ControlNet scales for normal or segmentation maps in architectural use was found.
- No independent fidelity evaluation of Decart Lucy Restyle or Krea Realtime on CAD/3D walkthrough input was found.
